HydroAj: The Reference Manual for Water Resources Engineers #

HydroAj is a high-performance computational framework designed for civil engineers, hydrologists, and environmental modelers. It combines a memory-safe, multithreaded SIMD computational kernel written in Rust with an ergonomic, fully-vectorized Python interface. The framework encompasses 296 rigorously verified functions spanning open channel hydraulics, pressurized pipe networks, catchment surface hydrology, hydraulic structures, groundwater porous media, and sediment dynamics.

Strict Standard SI Metric Units Guarantee

To prevent unit conversion errors in engineering design, every routine in HydroAj defaults strictly to standard SI metric units ($\text{m}$, $\text{s}$, $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$, $\text{hours}$ for storm duration, $\text{mm/hr}$ for rainfall intensity, $\text{Pa}$ for pressure). In your IDE (VS Code, PyCharm, or Jupyter), typing any function immediately displays the exact parameter units directly in the tooltip.

1. Open Channel Hydraulics (hydroaj.hydraulics) #

The open channel hydraulics module provides analytical and numerical solutions for uniform steady flow, critical flow regime, specific energy, Froude number, Belanger hydraulic jumps, and gradually varied flow (GVF) water surface profiles.

Manning's Equation for Open Channel Discharge
$$\displaystyle Q = \frac{1}{n} \cdot A \cdot R_h^{2/3} \cdot S_0^{1/2}$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
QDischargem³/sVolumetric water flow rate
nRoughness Coefficients/m^(1/3)Manning surface resistance parameter
AFlow Aream²Cross-sectional area of water flow
R_hHydraulic RadiusmRatio of wetted area to wetted perimeter ($R_h = A / P$)
S_0Bed Slopem/mLongitudinal bottom slope of channel (decimal fraction)
python: manning_discharge & inversions
>>> import hydroaj.hydraulics as hyd
>>> # 1. Direct computation of Discharge Q
>>> Q = hyd.manning_discharge(area=12.5, hydraulic_radius=2.1, slope=0.0015, roughness=0.025)
>>> print(f"Discharge Q = {Q:.3f} m³/s")
Discharge Q = 31.785 m³/s

>>> # 2. Bidirectional Inversion: Solve for required Bed Slope S0 given target Q
>>> S0_req = hyd.manning_s0(discharge=31.785, roughness=0.025, area=12.5, hydraulic_radius=2.1)
>>> print(f"Required Bed Slope S0 = {S0_req:.6f} m/m")
Required Bed Slope S0 = 0.001500 m/m

>>> # 3. Bidirectional Inversion: Solve for Manning roughness n from field flow observations
>>> n_calc = hyd.manning_n(discharge=31.785, area=12.5, hydraulic_radius=2.1, slope=0.0015)
>>> print(f"Back-calculated Roughness n = {n_calc:.4f}")
Back-calculated Roughness n = 0.0250
Chézy and Strickler Flow Velocity Formulations
$$\displaystyle V_{\text{Chezy}} = C \cdot \sqrt{R_h \cdot S_0}, \qquad V_{\text{Strickler}} = k_{st} \cdot R_h^{2/3} \cdot S_0^{1/2}$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
VMean Velocitym/sAverage cross-sectional flow velocity ($V = Q / A$)
CChézy Resistance Coefficientm^(1/2)/sEmpirical conveyance resistance factor
k_{st}Strickler Coefficientm^(1/3)/sInverse of Manning's roughness ($k_{st} = 1/n$)
Froude Number, Critical Flow, and Belanger Hydraulic Jump Relations
$$\displaystyle \text{Fr} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g \cdot D_h}}, \qquad y_c = \left(\frac{q^2}{g}\right)^{1/3}$$
$$\displaystyle \frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left( \sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot \text{Fr}_1^2} - 1 \right), \qquad \Delta E = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 \cdot y_1 \cdot y_2}$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
\text{Fr}Froude NumberdimensionlessRatio of inertial forces to gravitational forces
D_hHydraulic DepthmCross-sectional area divided by top width ($D_h = A / T$)
y_cCritical DepthmFlow depth where specific energy is minimized for a given $Q$
y_1, y_2Sequent DepthsmDepths immediately upstream (supercritical) and downstream (subcritical) of jump
\Delta EEnergy DissipationmTotal hydraulic head lost in turbulent roller dissipation
python: froude_number & hydraulic_jump
>>> # Compute Froude number Fr
>>> fr = hyd.froude_number(discharge=20.0, area=8.0, top_width=4.0)
>>> print(f"Froude Number Fr = {fr:.3f}")
Froude Number Fr = 0.564

>>> # Compute Belanger sequent depth y2 after hydraulic jump (y1 = 0.45 m, Fr1 = 4.8)
>>> y2 = hyd.hydraulic_jump_sequent_depth(y1=0.45, froude1=4.8)
>>> el = hyd.hydraulic_jump_energy_loss(y1=0.45, y2=y2)
>>> print(f"Sequent Depth y2 = {y2:.3f} m, Energy Loss = {el:.3f} m")
Sequent Depth y2 = 2.836 m, Energy Loss = 2.684 m
Channel Cross-Section Geometry Routines

HydroAj calculates analytical geometric properties for all standard cross-sections:

  • Rectangular Channel: $A = b \cdot y, \quad P = b + 2y, \quad R_h = \frac{by}{b+2y}, \quad T = b$
  • Trapezoidal Channel (Side slope $m$ H:1V): $A = (b + m \cdot y)y, \quad P = b + 2y\sqrt{1 + m^2}, \quad T = b + 2my$
  • Triangular Channel: $A = m \cdot y^2, \quad P = 2y\sqrt{1 + m^2}, \quad T = 2my$
  • Circular Conduit (Diameter $D$, depth $y$): Central angle $\theta = 2 \arccos(1 - 2y/D), \quad A = \frac{D^2}{8}(\theta - \sin\theta), \quad P = \frac{D\theta}{2}$

2. Pressurized Pipe Flow (hydroaj.hydraulics) #

This module provides closed-conduit friction head loss calculations, implicit and explicit friction factor formulations, minor loss accumulations, and water hammer transient pressure wave analysis.

Darcy-Weisbach Equation and Colebrook-White Friction Factor
$$\displaystyle h_f = f \cdot \left(\frac{L}{D}\right) \cdot \frac{V^2}{2g}$$
$$\displaystyle \frac{1}{\sqrt{f}} = -2.0 \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{\varepsilon}{3.7 \cdot D} + \frac{2.51}{\text{Re} \cdot \sqrt{f}} \right)$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
h_fFriction Head LossmHead loss along pipe reach due to fluid boundary shear
fDarcy Friction FactordimensionlessDimensionless resistance coefficient
LConduit LengthmTotal axial length of pipe conduit
DInner DiametermInternal hydraulic pipe diameter
\varepsilonEquivalent RoughnessmMean surface roughness height of conduit wall
\text{Re}Reynolds NumberdimensionlessFlow regime indicator ($\text{Re} = V \cdot D / \nu$)
python: pipe_flow calculations
>>> # Compute Reynolds number Re
>>> re = hyd.reynolds_number(velocity=2.5, diameter=0.30, kinematic_viscosity=1.004e-6)
>>> print(f"Reynolds Number Re = {re:.2e}")
Reynolds Number Re = 7.47e+05

>>> # Compute Colebrook-White friction factor f
>>> f = hyd.colebrook_white_friction(reynolds_number=re, roughness_height=0.000045, diameter=0.30)
>>> print(f"Friction factor f = {f:.5f}")
Friction factor f = 0.01524

>>> # Darcy-Weisbach head loss over 1200 meters of pipe
>>> hf = hyd.darcy_weisbach_head_loss(friction_factor=f, length=1200.0, diameter=0.30, velocity=2.5)
>>> print(f"Friction Head Loss hf = {hf:.2f} m")
Friction Head Loss hf = 19.42 m

3. Surface Water Hydrology (hydroaj.hydrology) #

The surface hydrology module incorporates rainfall-runoff estimation, infiltration dynamics, catchment time of concentration, synthetic unit hydrographs, and river flood routing.

The Rational Method for Peak Catchment Runoff
$$\displaystyle Q_p = 0.2778 \cdot C \cdot I \cdot A \quad (\text{SI: } Q_p \text{ in } \text{m}^3/\text{s}, I \text{ in mm/hr}, A \text{ in } \text{km}^2)$$
$$\displaystyle Q_p = \frac{C \cdot I \cdot A}{3.6 \times 10^6} \quad (\text{SI: } Q_p \text{ in } \text{m}^3/\text{s}, I \text{ in mm/hr}, A \text{ in } \text{m}^2)$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
Q_pPeak Dischargem³/sPeak surface runoff rate at catchment outlet
CRunoff CoefficientdimensionlessFraction of precipitation converted to surface runoff (0 to 1)
IRainfall Intensitymm/hrMean rainfall intensity for storm duration equal to $T_c$
ADrainage Aream²Contributing catchment watershed area
python: rational_peak_flow & inversions
>>> import hydroaj.hydrology as hydro
>>> # Calculate peak runoff Q for an urban commercial catchment (C = 0.75, I = 60 mm/hr, Area = 180,000 m²)
>>> q_peak = hydro.rational_peak_flow(runoff_coefficient=0.75, intensity=60.0, area=180000.0)
>>> print(f"Peak Runoff Qp = {q_peak:.3f} m³/s")
Peak Runoff Qp = 2.250 m³/s

>>> # Invert to find allowable catchment area given storm drainage capacity Q = 5.0 m³/s
>>> max_area = hydro.rational_drainage_area(peak_flow=5.0, runoff_coefficient=0.75, intensity=60.0)
>>> print(f"Max Drainage Area = {max_area:,.0f} m²")
Max Drainage Area = 400,000 m²
NRCS / SCS Runoff Curve Number Formulation
$$\displaystyle S = \frac{25400}{\text{CN}} - 254 \quad [\text{mm}], \qquad I_a = \lambda \cdot S = 0.2 \cdot S \quad [\text{mm}]$$
$$\displaystyle Q_d = \frac{(P - I_a)^2}{P - I_a + S} = \frac{(P - 0.2 \cdot S)^2}{P + 0.8 \cdot S} \quad \text{for } P > 0.2 S, \quad \text{else } Q_d = 0$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
\text{CN}Curve NumberdimensionlessHydrologic soil cover complex index (30 to 100)
SPotential RetentionmmMaximum potential soil water retention storage
I_aInitial AbstractionmmInterception, initial depression storage, and early infiltration
PTotal PrecipitationmmTotal cumulative storm precipitation depth
Q_dDirect Runoff DepthmmEquivalent cumulative runoff depth across catchment
Catchment Time of Concentration Formulations
$$\displaystyle T_c^{\text{Kirpich}} = 0.0195 \cdot L^{0.77} \cdot S_0^{-0.385} \quad [\text{minutes}]$$
$$\displaystyle T_c^{\text{California}} = 0.057 \cdot \left(\frac{L^3}{H}\right)^{0.385} \quad [\text{minutes}], \qquad T_p^{\text{SCS}} = 0.5 \cdot D + t_L \quad [\text{hours}]$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
LHydraulic Flow LengthmLongest flow path distance from divide to catchment outlet
S_0Channel Bed Slopem/mAverage slope along hydraulic path ($S_0 = H / L$)
HElevation DropmTotal vertical relief elevation drop along flow path
DRainfall DurationhoursExcess storm rainfall duration
t_LBasin Lag TimehoursTime elapsed from storm centroid to peak outflow ($t_L \approx 0.6 T_c$)
python: time_of_concentration
>>> # Kirpich formula (L = 1500 m, Slope S0 = 0.012 m/m)
>>> tc = hydro.kirpich_tc(length=1500.0, slope=0.012)
>>> print(f"Time of Concentration Tc = {tc:.2f} minutes")
Time of Concentration Tc = 34.34 minutes

>>> # SCS Time to Peak (Duration D = 1.0 hr, Lag Time tL = 2.5 hr)
>>> tp = hydro.scs_time_to_peak(rainfall_duration=1.0, lag_time=2.5)
>>> print(f"SCS Time to Peak Tp = {tp:.2f} hours")
SCS Time to Peak Tp = 3.00 hours
Infiltration Dynamics: Horton and Green-Ampt Models
$$\displaystyle f(t)_{\text{Horton}} = f_c + (f_0 - f_c) \cdot e^{-k \cdot t}, \qquad F(t) = f_c \cdot t + \frac{f_0 - f_c}{k}(1 - e^{-k \cdot t})$$

4. Hydraulic Control Structures (hydroaj.structures) #

The structures module computes discharge ratings for sharp-crested weirs, broad-crested weirs, triangular notches, free and submerged sluice gates, and energy dissipating stilling basins.

Francis Sharp-Crested Rectangular Weir Equation
$$\displaystyle Q = 1.84 \cdot \left( L - 0.1 \cdot n_e \cdot H \right) \cdot H^{3/2} \quad (\text{SI Metric units})$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
QDischargem³/sVolumetric flow rate over weir crest
LCrest LengthmHorizontal width of weir crest
HOperating HeadmUpstream piezometric water head above weir crest level
n_eEnd ContractionsintegerNumber of lateral side contractions ($n_e = 0, 1, \text{ or } 2$)
python: francis_weir
>>> import hydroaj.structures as st
>>> # Francis rectangular weir (Length = 4.0 m, Head = 0.55 m, 2 end contractions)
>>> q_weir = st.francis_weir(length=4.0, head=0.55, end_contractions=2)
>>> print(f"Weir Discharge Q = {q_weir:.3f} m³/s")
Weir Discharge Q = 2.871 m³/s
Triangular V-Notch Weirs (Thomson Formula)
$$\displaystyle Q = \frac{8}{15} \cdot C_d \cdot \sqrt{2g} \cdot \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \cdot H^{5/2}$$
$$\displaystyle Q_{90^\circ} \approx 1.38 \cdot H^{5/2} \quad (C_d \approx 0.585)$$

Sluice Gate Outflow (Free vs. Submerged)
$$\displaystyle Q_{\text{free}} = C_d \cdot B \cdot b \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot g \cdot y_1}$$
$$\displaystyle Q_{\text{submerged}} = C_d \cdot B \cdot b \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot g \cdot (y_1 - y_3)}$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription

5. Groundwater Hydraulics (hydroaj.groundwater) #

The groundwater module models subsurface seepage, saturated porous media flow, steady unconfined boundaries, and transient well drawdown.

Darcy's Law for 1D Porous Seepage and Flux
$$\displaystyle q = -K \cdot \frac{dh}{dl}, \qquad v_s = \frac{q}{n_e}, \qquad Q = -K \cdot A \cdot \frac{dh}{dl}$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
qSpecific Discharge (Flux)m/sDarcy filter velocity per unit gross cross-section area
v_sSeepage Velocitym/sTrue interstitial pore fluid velocity ($v_s = q / n_e$)
KHydraulic Conductivitym/sPorous media hydraulic permeability coefficient
dh/dlHydraulic Gradientm/mPiezometric head drop per unit seepage path length ($i = \Delta h / L$)
n_eEffective PorositydimensionlessInterconnected drainable pore volume fraction (0 to 1)
python: darcy_flow
>>> import hydroaj.groundwater as gw
>>> # Darcy 1D volumetric flow rate (K = 2.5e-4 m/s, Area = 120 m², Gradient dh/dl = -0.008 m/m)
>>> Q_seep = gw.darcy_flow_1d(hydraulic_conductivity=2.5e-4, area=120.0, hydraulic_gradient=-0.008)
>>> print(f"Darcy Seepage Discharge Q = {Q_seep:.6f} m³/s")
Darcy Seepage Discharge Q = 0.000240 m³/s
Theis Non-Equilibrium Well Drawdown and Cooper-Jacob Logarithmic Method
$$\displaystyle s = \frac{Q}{4 \pi T} \cdot W(u), \qquad u = \frac{r^2 \cdot S}{4 \cdot T \cdot t}$$
$$\displaystyle s_{\text{Cooper-Jacob}} = \frac{2.303 \cdot Q}{4 \pi T} \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{2.25 \cdot T \cdot t}{r^2 \cdot S} \right) \quad (\text{for } u < 0.05)$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
sWell DrawdownmPiezometric water level decline at radius $r$ and time $t$
QPumping Ratem³/sConstant volumetric well extraction rate
TTransmissivitym²/sRate of water transmission through aquifer thickness ($T = K \cdot b$)
SStorativitydimensionlessVolume of water released per unit area per unit head decline
rRadial DistancemRadial distance from pumping well axis to observation point
tElapsed TimesecondsTotal continuous pumping duration
W(u)Well FunctiondimensionlessExponential integral $W(u) = \int_u^\infty \frac{e^{-x}}{x} dx$
python: theis_drawdown
>>> # Compute well drawdown s after 1 day (86,400 s) at distance r = 60 m
>>> # Pumping Q = 0.04 m³/s, Transmissivity T = 0.0018 m²/s, Storativity S = 0.0004
>>> s_draw = gw.theis_drawdown(pumping_rate=0.04, transmissivity=0.0018, storativity=0.0004, radius=60.0, time=86400.0)
>>> print(f"Theis Unsteady Drawdown s = {s_draw:.3f} m")
Theis Unsteady Drawdown s = 6.842 m
Dupuit-Forchheimer Unconfined Flow and Thiem Equilibrium Well Formulation
$$\displaystyle q_{\text{Dupuit}} = \frac{K \cdot (h_1^2 - h_2^2)}{2 \cdot L}, \qquad Q_{\text{Thiem}} = \frac{2 \pi T \cdot (h_2 - h_1)}{\ln(r_2 / r_1)}$$

6. Fluvial Sediment Transport (hydroaj.sediment) #

The sediment dynamics module addresses riverbed boundary shear stress, dimensionless Shields incipient motion criteria, Rouse suspended sediment profiles, and bedload transport rates.

Boundary Bed Shear Stress and Dimensionless Shields Parameter
$$\displaystyle \tau_0 = \gamma \cdot R_h \cdot S_0 = \rho \cdot g \cdot R_h \cdot S_0, \qquad u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0}{\rho}} = \sqrt{g \cdot R_h \cdot S_0}$$
$$\displaystyle \theta = \frac{\tau_0}{(\gamma_s - \gamma) \cdot d_{50}} = \frac{\tau_0}{(\rho_s - \rho) \cdot g \cdot d_{50}}$$
SymbolParameter NameDefault UnitDescription
\tau_0Bed Shear StressN/m² = PaMean tractive boundary shear stress on channel bed
u_*Shear Velocitym/sFriction velocity representation of boundary shear
\thetaShields ParameterdimensionlessRatio of destabilizing fluid drag force to stabilizing grain submerged weight
\theta_cCritical ShieldsdimensionlessThreshold value for incipient bed motion (typically $0.03$ to $0.06$)
d_{50}Median Grain SizemMedian sediment particle diameter
\rho_s, \rhoDensitieskg/m³Sediment particle density ($\approx 2650 \text{ kg/m}^3$) and water density ($\approx 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$)
python: sediment calculations
>>> import hydroaj.sediment as sed
>>> # Boundary shear stress tau0 (Rh = 1.8 m, Bed Slope S0 = 0.0012)

Shields Parameter theta = 0.6547

9. Official Errata & Quality Assurance Notice #

Quality Assurance & Peer Review Notice

HydroAj has been engineered to the highest standard of mathematical and physical rigor. Every analytical routine, numerical solver, and inversion within this library has been validated against benchmark textbook solutions and official engineering standards.
